# The Gilded Ace: should we replace the 10 lowest machines?

## Problem
The GM wants to replace the 10 lowest-earning machines (Jan 1 to Jun 30, 2025). Low earnings can come from luck, location or a leak. We tested all three.

## Q1: which machines are truly underperforming?
- 0 of the GM's 10 are truly bad: 6 bad spot, 3 short exposure, 1 inspect.
- 9 sit in Back Forty, which earns 65% of the floor rate for the same games. During the walkway closure it earned 46% of the rest of the floor, against 67% before and 64% after.
- The casino already replaced one machine in slot L-38-5 on 2025-05-13, and the replacement landed on the list too: replacing doesn't fix a bad spot.
- M-1152 more than doubled its earnings per available hour after moving from Back Forty to Main Entrance (+106% vs unmoved machines). Only 4 machines moved.
- M-1158 is an inspect lead (hold 3.0% against 10.0% expected), a lead to check, not an accusation.
- Tests: 10,000 fair replays per machine for luck (a supported explanation for 5 of the 10, unlikely for 3, not supported for 2), and each machine against the same game elsewhere.

## Q2 to Q5
- **Demand:** coin-in changed -1.6% from the first to the last 4 full weeks.
- **Downtime:** 0.38% of scheduled hours, at most $90,663 of expected win. It does not explain the list.
- **Players:** tracked players lost $15.6M against $15.5M expected. The top 10% hold 66% of expected losses.
- **Comps:** $190.7k, rising from 1.11% to 1.32% of theo, Jan to Jun.

## Model
Weekday plus events misses daily coin-in by $50,920 on May and June, against $218,258 for a constant baseline (train Jan to Apr, test May to Jun). It changes where staffing goes: Saturday ($1.8M) and Friday ($1.6M) are forecast busiest, so an extra technician shift goes there, and the forecast is the baseline for measuring a promotion weekend's lift.
